# Success Criteria — Phase 1

## How We Define Success

Phase 1 is successful if the prototype validates enough core assumptions to justify investing in backend development. The metrics below are the minimum thresholds that need to be met before proceeding to Phase 2.

---

## Quantitative Targets

| Metric | Target | How Measured |
|---|---|---|
| Users correctly recall portion size and macro info after viewing PDP | 70%+ | Comprehension check question post-PDP |
| Users say they would trust claims enough to try a first order | 60%+ | Direct question in debrief |
| Users click a preset filter vs browsing all meals | 50%+ | Click tracking in Lovable |
| Users interact with the swap option before checkout | 40%+ | Click tracking in Lovable |
| Onboarding completion rate (no dropoffs) | 80%+ | Session observation |

---

## Qualitative Signals to Watch

Beyond the numbers, these qualitative signals indicate strong product-market fit potential:

- Users spontaneously mention the kitchen provenance block as a trust-builder (without being prompted)
- Users reference price-per-macro when explaining their meal selection decision
- Users express frustration or confusion at any specific flow step (signals redesign needed)
- Users ask "can I actually order this?" — indicates genuine purchase intent

---

## Decision Framework

| Outcome | Decision |
|---|---|
| All 5 quantitative targets met + strong qualitative signals | Proceed to Phase 2 backend development |
| 3-4 targets met with clear patterns on failures | Iterate prototype on failing flows, retest before Phase 2 |
| Fewer than 3 targets met | Revisit problem framing and core assumptions before any further investment |

---

## What Failure Would Tell Us

- Low PDP comprehension: Nutrition information design needs rework — too dense or not scannable
- Low trust score: Kitchen provenance and audit signals are not prominent enough or not credible
- Low filter usage: Filter categories do not map to how users think about their food choices
- Low swap engagement: Swap feature adds complexity without clear value — consider removing in Phase 2
- High onboarding dropout: Too many steps or consent copy is creating friction — simplify
