# Problem Statement — Meal Planner Concept

## Context

Healthy meal delivery is a growing market but suffers from a fundamental trust and clarity problem. Users who care about nutrition — gym-goers, diabetics, busy professionals, allergen-sensitive individuals — are underserved by apps that slap "healthy" on meals without transparency about what that actually means.

The result: low conversion, high churn, and users reverting to cooking at home or choosing convenience over health.

---

## Target Personas

| Persona | Key Need | Current Frustration |
|---|---|---|
| Busy professional | Quick, healthy lunch near office with no decision fatigue | Too many choices, unclear nutrition |
| Gym-goer | High protein, clear macros, value for money | No price-per-macro visibility, vague portion sizes |
| Diabetic / health-conscious | Low carb, allergen-safe, trustworthy sourcing | Generic "healthy" labels with no clinical backing |
| Allergen-sensitive user | Clear allergen labeling, zero ambiguity | Inconsistent labeling, fear of hidden ingredients |

---

## Core Problems

**1. Vague "healthy" claims with no proof**
Users see "healthy" tags with no context — no macros, no ingredients, no sourcing information. Trust is low.

**2. Poor portion clarity**
Meal images look large, portions arrive small. Users feel deceived and do not reorder.

**3. No value framing for health-conscious buyers**
Price is shown per meal but health-conscious users think in terms of protein, carbs, and calories. Price-per-macro is never surfaced.

**4. Choice overload**
Large menus with no smart filtering or persona-based presets overwhelm users, especially first-time visitors.

**5. No kitchen transparency**
Users have no idea where food is prepared, when it was last audited, or whether hygiene standards are maintained.

---

## Opportunity

Build a meal discovery and ordering experience that leads with nutrition transparency, trust signals, and smart personalization — making it easier for health-conscious users to find, trust, and order the right meal for their goals.

---

## Hypothesis

If we surface verified nutrition data, kitchen provenance, price-per-macro value framing, and persona-based plan presets — conversion intent and trust scores will improve significantly among health-conscious segments compared to a generic meal delivery experience.
